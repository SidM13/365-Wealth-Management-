Specialized Investment Funds (SIF)

A SEBI-regulated investment category for eligible investors seeking strategies beyond conventional mutual-fund structures, offered by qualifying mutual-fund houses under a separate SIF framework.

Understand SIF Opportunities
What Is a SIF?

Between mutual funds and portfolio management services

SEBI introduced the SIF framework to give investors access to more flexible investment strategies through a regulated, pooled structure managed by eligible Asset Management Companies.

SIF strategies may use approaches not generally available in conventional mutual-fund schemes. Examples are long-short positions in equity, debt or hybrid portfolios, and the limited use of derivatives for purposes beyond hedging, within limits set by SEBI. SIFs carry their own branding, distinct from the AMC's mutual-fund schemes, and are subject to a higher minimum investment threshold prescribed by SEBI.

Because the strategies are more complex, SIFs are intended for investors who understand them and can bear their risks. They are not suitable for every investor.

Role in a Portfolio

A complement to a diversified core, not a replacement

Strategy Diversification

Some SIF strategies aim to behave differently from long-only funds, which may add a different source of returns and risk to a portfolio.

For Eligible Investors

Typically considered by experienced investors with a sufficiently large, already-diversified portfolio and a clear understanding of the strategy.

Distinct Risks

Derivative positions, short exposure, strategy and manager risk, and potentially different liquidity or redemption terms.

SIF eligibility & risk disclosure

Specialized Investment Funds are regulated by SEBI and offered by eligible AMCs. Investment is subject to the minimum investment amount and eligibility conditions prescribed by SEBI from time to time and to the terms of the Investment Strategy Information Document (ISID). SIF strategies may involve derivatives and short positions, can be more volatile than conventional mutual funds, and may result in loss of capital. They are not suitable for all investors. Returns are not guaranteed. Mutual Fund investments are subject to market risks, read all scheme related documents carefully. Distribution of SIFs is subject to applicable certification and empanelment requirements.
