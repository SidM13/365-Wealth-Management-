SIP, Simply Explained

Investing becomes a habit, not a decision you make each month

Instead of trying to time the market, a SIP invests the same amount on a fixed date. When prices are lower, the same amount buys more units; when prices are higher, it buys fewer.

Over long periods, regular investing combined with the growth of your earlier investments, known as compounding, can help build wealth for long-term goals. Outcomes depend on the market and the scheme, and are not guaranteed.

Key Concepts

Why investors choose SIPs

Disciplined Investing

Automated investing keeps you on track, regardless of market noise.

Regular Investment

A fixed amount on a fixed date aligns investing with your monthly cash flow.

Rupee-Cost Averaging

Buying across market levels averages your purchase cost over time.

Power of Compounding

Returns earned can themselves generate returns over long periods.

Long-Term Goals

Map SIPs to goals such as education, retirement or wealth creation.

Start Manageably

Begin with amounts that fit your budget; many schemes allow small SIPs.

Step Up as Income Grows

Increase your SIP each year to keep pace with income and goals.

Flexibility

Pause, modify or stop SIPs as per scheme terms (exit loads may apply).
